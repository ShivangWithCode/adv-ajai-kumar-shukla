Frequently Asked Legal Questions

Important procedural insights regarding High Court filings, bail remedies, and client confidentiality.

⚖ What is the procedure for filing a Writ Petition in the High Court Lucknow Bench?

Under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, an extraordinary writ petition is filed before the Hon’ble High Court when a citizen's fundamental or statutory rights are violated by the State, executive authorities, or statutory corporations. The process involves:

  1. Procuring certified copies of impugned administrative orders, FIRs, or departmental decisions.
  2. Drafting a comprehensive petition outlining the chronological list of dates, violations of legal rights, and grounds for judicial intervention.
  3. Affidavit swearing at the High Court Oaths Commission and filing in the High Court Reporting Section.
  4. Listing before the designated Single or Division Bench for preliminary hearing, stay adjudication, and issuance of notice to the opposite parties.
📋 What essential documents should a client bring for the initial consultation?

For an effective and accurate preliminary evaluation, it is recommended to carry the following complete documentation:

  • For Criminal Matters: Certified copy of the FIR, General Diary (GD) entry, rejection order from the Sessions Court (for High Court bail), charge sheet, and arrest memo (if any).
  • For Civil & Property Matters: Registered sale deeds, khatauni/khasra revenue records, partition deeds, previous civil court decrees, and municipal notices.
  • For Service Grievances: Appointment letter, seniority lists, departmental show-cause notices, inquiry reports, and adverse transfer/suspension orders.
  • Identity Proof: Valid government ID (Aadhaar/Voter Card) of the petitioner or authorized parokar.
🔒 Is my communication with the chamber protected under Advocate-Client Privilege?

Yes, absolutely. Under Section 126 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (and corresponding provisions in the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam), all communications, consultations, documents, and disclosures made by a client to their advocate in the course of and for the purpose of professional engagement are strictly privileged and confidential.

Adv. Ajai Kumar Shukla's chamber adheres strictly to the highest ethical codes of the Bar Council of Uttar Pradesh and will never disclose your confidential case facts to any third party without your express written authorization.

⚡ What is the distinction between Anticipatory Bail and Regular Bail in Uttar Pradesh?

In Uttar Pradesh, statutory bail provisions operate under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) / Code of Criminal Procedure:

  • Anticipatory Bail (Pre-Arrest): Applied under Section 482 BNSS (formerly Section 438 CrPC) before an arrest takes place, when an individual reasonably apprehends arrest in a non-bailable offense. It shields the person from custodial detention upon furnishing requisite sureties.
  • Regular Bail (Post-Arrest): Applied under Section 483 BNSS (formerly Section 439 CrPC) after the accused has been arrested or surrendered before the magistrate and is in police or judicial custody.

Our chamber prioritizes urgent anticipatory bail petitions before the Sessions Court and High Court Lucknow to safeguard individual liberty against mala fide complaints.

🏛 What standards of conduct are governed by the Bar Council Disciplinary Committee?

The Disciplinary Committee of the Bar Council of Uttar Pradesh exercises quasi-judicial statutory powers under Section 35 and 36 of the Advocates Act, 1961. It adjudicates charges of professional misconduct, breach of client trust, conflict of interest, and moral integrity.

Having served as a Member of the Disciplinary Committee, Bar Council of UP, Adv. Ajai Kumar Shukla upholds an unblemished standard of transparency, meticulous filing, and zero tolerance for unprofessional conduct throughout his practice.
